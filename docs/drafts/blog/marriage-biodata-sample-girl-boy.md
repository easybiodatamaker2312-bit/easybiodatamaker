A marriage biodata sample is useful when you know the information you want to share but are unsure about order and wording. A good sample should show structure rather than pretend that one format is correct for every family. The same core sections can work for a bride or groom, while education, career, lifestyle and expectations are filled with the actual person’s information.

**Age:** 28
**Height:** 5'4" (163 cm)
**Current city:** Ahmedabad, Gujarat
**Education:** MBA, Finance
**Occupation:** Finance Manager
**About:** Aditi enjoys reading, weekend travel and spending time with family. She values clear communication and a balanced professional and personal life.
**Family:** Father — business; Mother — homemaker; Brother — software professional in Bengaluru
**Expectations:** Looking for a kind, communicative partner who respects family relationships and supports each other’s career goals.

This is fictional sample data. A real biodata should use verified information and only include fields the candidate is comfortable sharing.

## What should be different between a girl and boy biodata?

There does not need to be a completely different information architecture. Both profiles can contain personal details, education, career, family, About Me, expectations, photo and contact information. The actual content should reflect the person rather than forcing gender-specific stereotypes.

For example, income should not automatically be displayed because a profile is for a man, nor should hobbies or career ambitions be minimized because a profile is for a woman. If a family expects a particular field, it can be added as an optional field.

## Three sample styles

**Simple one-page sample:** personal details, photo, education, career, family and expectations. This is useful for WhatsApp forwarding and first introductions.

**Modern sample:** prominent photo, short profile summary, career/location, lifestyle interests and a compact family section. This can work well when the candidate wants a contemporary presentation.

**Traditional sample:** personal details, family, community or horoscope fields, education/career and expectations. Use this only when those traditional fields are genuinely relevant to the family.

## Common mistakes when copying samples

Do not copy another person's salary, job title, family description or horoscope details just because they appear in a sample. Sample text should demonstrate structure, not become factual content for the new profile.

Another mistake is writing an excessively long About Me. Three or four specific sentences are often more useful than a page of adjectives. Expectations should also be realistic and respectful. Avoid turning them into an exclusion list.

Privacy is equally important. A sample should never encourage publishing Aadhaar numbers, passport numbers, exact home addresses, bank information or confidential employment details. A matrimonial biodata is often forwarded, so the first version should contain only information needed for a meaningful introduction.

## Final review checklist

Before sharing, verify name, age/date of birth, height, education, occupation, city, family names and contact information. Confirm that the photo is current. Remove sensitive information that does not belong in the first introduction. Finally, read the document on a phone to make sure the important facts can be found quickly.

A useful girl or boy marriage biodata sample should make the next conversation easier. It should not try to make a person sound perfect. Accurate details, a natural introduction and clear expectations are more valuable than decorative claims.
